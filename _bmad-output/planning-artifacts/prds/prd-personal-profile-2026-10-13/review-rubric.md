# PRD Quality Review — Personal Profile (1health), NG-9019

## Overall verdict
This is a solid, honest brownfield PRD. The personas each drive decisions, the glossary is real, most FRs have concrete and testable consequences taken from the legacy behaviour, and deferring the Medical tab is handled openly. The risks sit in the new behaviour layered on top of parity, which the PRD specifies less tightly than the parity behaviour. Local draft persistence of PHI is not bound to a user or time-limited. Multi-step saves (insurance plus card uploads, stale drafts) have no defined failure semantics. A 16-day runway to 2026-10-29 for the full scope is never discussed as a trade-off. Fix the critical and high findings before stories are cut. The rest can be resolved during UX and architecture.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
Most decisions are stated as decisions: zero social links (FR-24, "This changes today's behaviour"), non-atomic Save all (FR-5, "Saves are not atomic across Sections"), Medical as Epic 2 (§4.9), and the rollback path (§6). The Open Questions in §9 are genuinely open and each has an owner. The PRD does not dodge its choices.

What is missing is the cost side of the biggest calls. The header commits to "Target release: 2026.07.01 'Conduit', 2026-10-29". That date is 16 days after the PRD was created. The memlog records "Scope for 2026-10-29: everything". The PRD still adds several net-new capabilities (Save all, draft restore, SSN reveal, demote-offer flows) on top of full parity, and it never says what gets cut if the date holds. FR-2's local drafts are a real privacy trade-off for a healthcare app. They are presented as a feature, and the risk side is not named.

### Findings
- **[critical]** Draft persistence of PHI is not bound to a user and has no expiry (§4.1 FR-2, §5 NFR-1). FR-2 says unsaved edits are "kept locally and offered back after relaunch on the same device". NFR-1 says drafts "are cleared when they are saved or discarded". As written, a shared or family device can offer user A's unsaved demographics, insurance member IDs or subscriber details to user B after B launches the app. A draft that is never restored also stays on the device indefinitely. This is a launch-stakes healthcare app, and engineers implementing the text literally would ship a PHI exposure. *Fix:* require drafts to be keyed to the Launching user's id and restored only for that user. Add a TTL (for example 24 h). Clear drafts on explicit "Return to 1health" or sign-out. State the storage class (sessionStorage vs localStorage) and whether drafts are encrypted. Name the residual risk in a `[NOTE FOR PM]`.
- **[high]** The schedule vs scope trade-off is not acknowledged (header line 10; §6 Rollout). The release is 16 days out, with 32 FRs, a cross-repo cutover dependency (§6 "1health-fe cutover … is a release dependency") and External Application registration in two environments. There is no cut line and no priority ordering. *Fix:* add a `[NOTE FOR PM]` listing which capabilities are deferrable without breaking parity (candidates: FR-5 Save all, FR-2 draft restore, FR-13 Reveal, the FR-29 demote offer), and make SM-1 the explicit go/no-go gate for cutover.
- **[medium]** SSN Reveal is a new PHI exposure with no security decision recorded (§4.3 FR-13). Legacy has "No SSN reveal on own profile" (research-1health-fe.md). The PRD adds Reveal with a 30 s auto-hide but does not decide on re-authentication, audit logging of reveals, or rate limits. A security reviewer will ask these questions. *Fix:* state the decision (for example "no re-auth in v1; platform audit of `/ssn/expose` is relied on") and tag it `[ASSUMPTION]` if not confirmed.
- **[medium]** The decision to show the Medical tab as "Coming soon" is unexamined (§4.9 FR-32). Legacy hard-codes the tab as `disabled`. The PRD makes it visible, with an empty state that "describes what will appear there", but §9 Q1 says that content is unknown. Hiding the tab until Epic 2 was the alternative and is not mentioned. *Fix:* either supply the placeholder copy or change it to generic copy, and record why visible beats hidden (or vice versa).

## Substance over theater — strong
There are four personas, and each anchors a distinct journey with a real edge case (UJ-1 unverified primary, UJ-2 primary-per-type, UJ-3 signature removal, UJ-4 primary-address delete). None is decorative. The Vision is specific to this product: it names the retiring `/profile`, the three experience changes, and the External Application pattern. Most NFRs carry thresholds: WCAG 2.1 AA, 360 px, 2.5 s, named browsers. The JTBD list maps cleanly to features.

### Findings
- **[low]** NFR-4 uses an adjective where it needs a bound (§5 NFR-4). "Within 2.5 s on a typical broadband connection" gives no percentile, network profile or measurement point. *Fix:* use for example "p75 LCP ≤ 2.5 s measured from launch redirect, on a 10 Mbps / 40 ms RTT profile".

## Strategic coherence — adequate
The thesis is clear: parity plus "constraints explained before the user runs into them" plus "proves the External Application model" (§1). Feature scope follows from it, and the counter-metrics SM-C1 and SM-C2 are well chosen. They target exactly the ways SM-2 could be gamed.

The Success Metrics validate delivery more than the thesis. SM-1 is a launch gate ("100% of today's capabilities are mapped to FRs" is a property of this document, not an outcome). SM-4 is an engineering task. Only SM-2 and SM-3 touch the UX bet, and neither can be measured as specified.

### Findings
- **[medium]** No instrumentation requirement supports the metrics (§8 SM-2, SM-3, SM-C1; §5 NFR-1). SM-2 ("98% of Section saves succeed on the first attempt") and SM-C1 ("Save all with ≥1 failure" rate) need save telemetry. NFR-1 forbids PHI in analytics, and no FR defines PHI-free events. SM-3 says "Fewer … tickets" with no baseline count, target delta, or ticket-tagging method. *Fix:* add an FR or NFR for PHI-free telemetry events (section id, outcome, error code). Give SM-3 a numeric target and name who tags tickets.
- **[medium]** The metrics don't test the UX thesis (§8). The differentiator in §1 ("Constraints are explained before the user runs into them") could be measured directly, for example the share of contact/address saves rejected by platform 400s for Primary/duplicate rules, which should approach zero. SM-2's clause "Validation errors are caught on the client, not by the platform" gestures at this but is not a metric. *Fix:* turn that clause into SM-2b with a threshold.
- **[low]** "Proves the External Application model" has no requirement behind it (§1 last paragraph). Nothing in §4–§8 defines what "sets the pattern for others" produces (reusable launch/session module, documented template). *Fix:* add a deliverable or drop the claim.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Most parity FRs are excellent, because they inherit concrete legacy rules: FR-12 age bounds and Proctor rule, FR-16 US 10-digit rule, FR-26 duplicate key, FR-29 field minimums. FR-5's partial-failure behaviour is a model of a testable consequence.

The weak spots are the new behaviours and multi-step operations. Those are where story writers will have to guess.

### Findings
- **[high]** Draft restore has no conflict rule (§4.1 FR-2). FR-2 is headed "handles expiry gracefully", which is exactly the red-flag phrasing. If the record changed on the server between draft and relaunch (another device, legacy `/profile` during rollout, a care team edit), the PRD doesn't say whether the draft overwrites, merges, or is shown as a diff. *Fix:* specify, for example: "the draft is offered only if the server version of that Section is unchanged since the draft was taken; otherwise the user sees both and chooses."
- **[high]** Insurance save is two-step with no partial-failure semantics (§4.8 FR-29; addendum §A FR-29). The policy POST/PUT is followed by separate front/back card uploads. If the policy saves and an upload fails, the PRD doesn't say what the user sees, whether the drawer stays open, or whether a retry re-sends only the images. UJ-2's climax ("card shows … both card images") depends on this. *Fix:* add consequences for "policy saved, image upload failed", including the message, retry scope and the card's state.
- **[medium]** The "demote" target is undefined (§4.8 FR-29; UJ-2 edge case). "Offers to demote the other one" doesn't say to which precedence (Secondary? the user's choice?) or whether the demotion and the new save are one action. *Fix:* name the target precedence or require the user to choose it, and state the save order.
- **[medium]** The refresh trigger for the Verified badge is unspecified (§4.4 FR-17). "The badge updates the next time the Section is refreshed." In UJ-1 Maria verifies via SMS, possibly on another device, then returns. Nothing says what refreshes the Section (window focus, polling, manual control). *Fix:* add, for example: "Contact Sections re-fetch on window focus and offer a manual refresh."
- **[medium]** Social link editing contradicts the Non-Goals (§4.6 FR-24 vs §7 bullet 3 and the §3 Contact Point definition). FR-24 says users can "add, edit and remove" links. §7 says changing a saved Contact Point's value in place is a non-goal ("remove and re-add"), and the Glossary defines social-media entries as Contact Points. *Fix:* either state that "edit" is remove-and-re-add under the hood, or carve Social Media out of the §7 rule explicitly.
- **[medium]** The Verified state of an address after a manual edit is undefined (§4.7 FR-26). The PRD says picking a suggestion "marks the address Verified". It doesn't say whether editing line 1, city or zip afterwards (or editing a saved verified address) clears Verified. *Fix:* add a consequence such as "any manual change to line 1, city, zip or country clears Verified."
- **[medium]** The scope of Save all and of the tab-switch prompt is ambiguous (§4.1 FR-3, FR-5). Save all is "page-level", but FR-3 makes the user resolve unsaved changes before leaving a tab. It is unclear whether dirty Sections can exist on a non-visible tab, and so whether Save all (N) ever counts them. *Fix:* state that dirty state is per tab and Save all covers the active tab only, or that dirty Sections persist across tabs and FR-3 does not prompt.
- **[medium]** Error mapping is "where possible" (§4.1 FR-7). "Platform validation errors are mapped to human-readable messages … where possible" is untestable. Addendum §B already lists the known platform rules (duplicate, 20-limit, primary rules, email owned by another user), and `SOCIAL_SECURITY_NUMBER_EXISTS` is known. *Fix:* reference a finite error catalogue (known codes → message → field) and define the fallback for unknown codes.
- **[low]** The launch timing and environment fallback are vague (§4.1 FR-1). "Within one page load" isn't a bound. "If it can't be detected, the user can choose it manually" lets a user pick demo vs production, and the security/UX implications (sending a production token to demo?) aren't addressed. *Fix:* bound FR-1 in seconds (or defer to NFR-4), and say whether the manual choice exists in production builds.
- **[low]** "On blur" is ambiguous (§4.3 FR-13). It's unclear what loses focus to hide the SSN: the field, the Reveal control, or the window. *Fix:* name the element.
- **[low]** User-supplied SVG card images (§4.8 FR-29). Accepting SVG and rendering it full-size (FR-31) is an XSS vector if rendered inline. *Fix:* require that SVGs are rendered only via `<img>`/object URL, or rasterised, never inlined.

## Scope honesty — adequate
The Non-Goals in §7 do real work. Patient Chart, account settings and native apps are all explicitly out. Medical is deferred with a `[NOTE FOR PM]` placed at a real tension. Open-items density is low (2 OQs, 1 assumption, 1 NOTE) and acceptable for launch stakes, since neither OQ blocks the build.

The weakness is the parity framing. §4's preamble says FRs keep today's behaviour "unless they say otherwise", but only FR-24 actually says so.

### Findings
- **[medium]** Departures from parity are not labelled (§1 "functional parity"; §4 preamble). Net-new or changed behaviours are not marked as such: FR-13 Reveal (absent in legacy), FR-5 Save all, FR-2 drafts, FR-3 URL tab and deep-link, FR-29 demote-offer and "6000×4000 px" limit (not in research-1health-fe.md), FR-32 visible-not-disabled Medical tab. QA running SM-1 parity checks will not know which differences are intended. *Fix:* add a "New vs today" tag on each such FR, or a short parity-delta table in §1.
- **[low]** Unconfirmed inferences are under-tagged (§5 NFR-4, NFR-7; FR-8 360 px; FR-29 image dimensions). These read as invented thresholds, not confirmed requirements. *Fix:* tag them `[ASSUMPTION]` and index them in §10, or note their source.
- **[low]** Behaviour outside the default tenant is unspecified (§2.2). Users are defined as "acting in the default (patient) tenant", but the PRD doesn't say what happens if the app is launched while the user is in a tenant context. Legacy hides the sidebar item there, but External App registration may not. *Fix:* add an FR-1 consequence for a non-default-tenant launch, or a §6 registration constraint.

## Downstream usability — adequate
The glossary is present and mostly respected. FR (1–32), NFR (1–7), SM (1–4, C1–C2) and UJ (1–5) IDs are contiguous and unique. Every feature cross-references its UJ, and addendum §A gives a clean FR→endpoint map for architecture.

Two gaps will cost UX and story writers time.

### Findings
- **[medium]** The assignment of Sections to tabs is never stated (§4.1 Description; §4.2–§4.8). §4.1 names three tabs, but nowhere does the PRD say that Personal Information, Phone, Email, Social Media and Addresses live on Contacts. UJ-3 ("They open Personal Information") has no tab, and the header is "above three tabs". This is inferable from research files only. *Fix:* add one line to §4.1 mapping each Section to its tab.
- **[medium]** "Primary" is used as both a label and a flag (§3 Contact Label vs Primary; addendum §B). The Glossary lists "Primary" as an example Contact Label and also defines Primary as the single preferred Contact Point. Addendum §B uses a separate `primary` boolean. Downstream will not know whether choosing the label "Primary" makes something Primary. *Fix:* clarify in §3 whether "Primary" as a label is still offered, hidden, or synonymous with the flag.
- **[low]** UJ-3 and UJ-4 omit **Entry state** (§2.3), unlike UJ-1 and UJ-2. NG-9020 is never mapped to a feature, and §4.1 carries no Jira tag. *Fix:* add entry states, and tag §4.1 with its sub-task.

## Shape fit — strong
This is an external, user-facing, brownfield app feeding UX → architecture → stories, and the shape matches. UJs with named protagonists are load-bearing. The brownfield "Today" convention is the right device, though it needs consistent application (see Scope honesty). Technical detail is correctly pushed to `addendum.md`. The Medical placeholder is sized appropriately rather than padded with speculative FRs. No over- or under-formalization was found.

## Mechanical notes
- **Glossary/requirement drift:** FR-2 excludes "SSN and Signature drawings" from drafts. NFR-1 says drafts "exclude the SSN" only. Align NFR-1 to include Signature.
- **Limit inconsistency:** FR-16 says "at most 20 phones". Addendum §B says "At most 20 contact points per type", and Mobile and Landline are separate types, so the limit could be 40 phones. FR-20 omits the email limit entirely.
- **Brownfield accuracy:** §3 describes Mobile vs Landline as "the phone's mobile flag". research-backend.md notes "No isMobile flag" and that they are distinct types. The UI checkbox maps to type. The wording is fine for UX but may mislead architecture.
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip:** 1 inline `[ASSUMPTION]` (FR-14) ↔ 1 indexed. The roundtrip is clean. The "Confirmed" list is a good practice.
- **IDs and cross-references:** all FR/UJ/SM references resolve. SM-1 cites "UJ-1 to UJ-4", which correctly excludes UJ-5.
- **Required sections:** all present for launch stakes. No acceptance section is needed, because FR consequences carry it.
